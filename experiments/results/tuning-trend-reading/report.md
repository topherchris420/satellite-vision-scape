# What does the tuning task charge an agent that cannot read the match trend?

<sub>Experiment `tuning-trend-reading` · generated by `bun run experiment report tuning-trend-reading` from the trace files below · complete</sub>

## Question

If an agent cannot infer from the match meter which way the reference lies, how much does terminal tuning cost it, and does it still finish?

## Hypothesis

Reading the trend is worth a lot but is not required: a policy that ignores the match trend (sweeping one way and reversing only at the end of the dial) still locks all four terminals, because graded turns shrink near the reference, but it needs at least 10 more tuning inputs than the trend-reading policy.

**We would reconsider it if:**

- sweep fails to lock the terminals (the skill is required, not just valuable)
- sweep costs fewer than 10 extra inputs (the skill is barely worth an assist)

**It is supported if:**

- sweep locks all four terminals in every run, at a cost of at least 10 more inputs

## Why we asked

trend_inference is one of the three assists added after run A. It spells out the conclusion "the reference lies the other way". Before asking whether a model still needs it, measure how much the task rewards the underlying skill at all.

- src/server/agent/assists.ts: trend_inference, probes terminal-reverse-after-overshoot and terminal-continue-while-rising
- evals/results/2026-09-27-baseline.json: the baseline itself fails terminal-continue-while-rising cold (it steers from memory)

## Design

- **Independent variable:** `tuningDirection`: The baseline's terminal direction rule: `trend` (reverse when the match falls), `sweep` (cannot read the trend; starts towards the middle of the dial) or `blind` (cannot read the trend and always starts up).
- **Held fixed:** `provider` = `baseline`, `tuningAmount` = `graded`, `latency` = `run-b`
- **Episode:** from the start of After Hours until all four terminals are locked (at most 600 simulated s)
- **Runs:** 10 per condition; run *i* of every condition uses seed 1 + *i* (paired)

| Condition | Setting | Provider | Runs | Repeats exactly? |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | --: | :-- |
| `trend` Reverses when the match falls | `tuningDirection` = `trend` | baseline | 10 / 10 | no (seeded or live) |
| `sweep` Cannot read the trend: sweeps to the end of the dial | `tuningDirection` = `sweep` | baseline | 10 / 10 | no (seeded or live) |
| `blind` Cannot read the trend and ignores where the dial starts: always turns up first | `tuningDirection` = `blind` | baseline | 10 / 10 | no (seeded or live) |

## Limitations

- A scripted stand-in for a missing skill: a real model without the skill may fail differently (for example by oscillating instead of sweeping).
- Terminal start positions are fixed by the game, so the sweep's cost depends on which side of the reference each dial starts.
- For the four current terminals, 'turn towards the middle' is always the right first direction (src/game/afterhours/puzzle.ts, TuningSession start), so the terminal stage only exercises trend reading after an overshoot.
- The scripted baseline is a hand-written policy, not a model: its results describe what the world does to that policy, not what any model would do.
- Metrics are computed in the client from the session's own records; nothing here is server-attested.
