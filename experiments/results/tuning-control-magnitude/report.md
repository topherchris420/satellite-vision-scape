# Is a control bigger than the target zone enough to reproduce run A's oscillation?

<sub>Experiment `tuning-control-magnitude` · generated by `bun run experiment report tuning-control-magnitude` from the trace files below · complete</sub>

## Question

Does always turning a terminal dial with a long hold (run A's habit) make tuning oscillate, even for a policy that reads the match trend correctly?

## Hypothesis

Control size alone decides whether terminal tuning converges. A policy that follows the match trend perfectly but always uses a long hold (≈ 20% of the dial, wider than the ≈ 7% aligned zone) will overshoot again and again and fail to lock the terminals within the episode; the same policy with graded turns will lock all four.

**We would reconsider it if:**

- long locks all four terminals as reliably as graded: control size alone does not explain run A, and the direction/hold-still half deserves the attention
- long overshoots fewer than 10 more times than graded (median, paired)

**It is supported if:**

- long fails to lock all four terminals in most seeded runs, with many overshoots, while graded locks all four
- tap converges, but only by paying many more inputs than graded

## Why we asked

Live run A stopped after 473 decisions alternating dial up / dial down at the Harmony terminal. The fix for run B added three assists at once (trend_inference, hold_still_rule, control_magnitudes), so the run cannot say which one mattered. This experiment isolates the control-size half of the failure with a scripted policy, where the direction rule is held perfect.

- docs/NEXT_MODEL.md §1: run A, 473 decisions oscillating at Harmony
- src/game/afterhours/puzzle.ts: aligned tolerance 0.035 (≈ 7% of the dial wide), a 0.9 s hold ramps to ≈ 20%
- scripts/capture-probes.ts: the heavy-handed (always long) baseline was needed to produce the overshoot probe

## Design

- **Independent variable:** `tuningAmount`: The baseline's terminal control size: `graded`, or always `tap`, `short` or `long`.
- **Held fixed:** `provider` = `baseline`, `tuningDirection` = `trend`, `latency` = `run-b`
- **Episode:** from the start of After Hours until all four terminals are locked (at most 600 simulated s)
- **Runs:** 10 per condition; run *i* of every condition uses seed 1 + *i* (paired)

## Limitations

- A scripted policy with a perfect direction rule, not a model: it isolates what the task does to one habit, not whether Jev has the habit.
- Latency is resampled from one live run's 212 recorded latencies.
- The scripted baseline is a hand-written policy, not a model: its results describe what the world does to that policy, not what any model would do.
- Metrics are computed in the client from the session's own records; nothing here is server-attested.
