# Are route reviews every 3 s paying for anything?

<sub>Experiment `travel-review-interval` · generated by `bun run experiment report travel-review-interval` from the trace files below · complete</sub>

## Question

The runtime re-asks the provider every 3 s while it travels. In run B, 79 such reviews changed nothing. Would reviewing every 6 s save calls without changing outcomes?

## Hypothesis

Stretching the travel review from 3 s to 6 s cuts provider requests by at least 20 per journey and changes neither completion, total time (within 5 s) nor the coffee (within 2 points).

**We would reconsider it if:**

- fewer than 20 requests saved (reviews are not where the calls go)
- completion, time (> 5 s) or coffee (> 2 points) changes: reviews are doing work

**It is supported if:**

- at least 20 fewer requests per journey with no change in completion, time or coffee

## Why we asked

Run B: 79 of Jev's 212 decisions (37%) were mid-route reviews and 0 of 79 changed the plan. The scripted baseline spends 76 of its 155 calls the same way. NEXT_MODEL.md makes 6 s a tripwire at < 1% plan changes over ≥ 5 live runs; this experiment measures the other half of that trade, what reviews cost and what dropping half of them changes in the world.

- bun run trace docs/traces/jev-after-hours-live-2026-09-27.json: 79 reviews kept the plan, 0 replaced a running action
- docs/NEXT_MODEL.md §4: tripwire, reviews every 6 s

## Design

- **Independent variable:** `reviewMs`: How often the runtime re-asks while a travel intent runs (product default 3000).
- **Held fixed:** `provider` = `baseline`, `latency` = `run-b`
- **Episode:** from the start of After Hours until the midnight transmission is received (at most 900 simulated s)
- **Runs:** 10 per condition; run *i* of every condition uses seed 1 + *i* (paired)

## Limitations

- The scripted baseline rarely changes its mind, so this measures the cost of reviews and the effect of fewer reviews on the world, not how often a model would use a review to correct itself. The tripwire in NEXT_MODEL.md still needs ≥ 5 live runs for that.
- Does not change the product default: this is evidence for the tripwire, not the tripwire.
- The scripted baseline is a hand-written policy, not a model: its results describe what the world does to that policy, not what any model would do.
- Metrics are computed in the client from the session's own records; nothing here is server-attested.
