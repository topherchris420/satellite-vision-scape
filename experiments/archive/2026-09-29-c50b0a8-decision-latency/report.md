# Does decision latency change what happens in the world?

<sub>Experiment `decision-latency` · generated by `bun run experiment report decision-latency` from the trace files below · complete</sub>

## Question

Jev answered in 289 ms (median) in run B. Would a slower model do worse at After Hours, or merely take longer?

## Hypothesis

At run B's latency the world is insensitive to decision speed. At four times run B's latency the whole journey takes measurably longer (at least 10 s), but completion and the coffee are unchanged, because the executor, not the decision loop, does the time-critical control.

**We would reconsider it if:**

- run-b already costs more than 10 s (latency matters at today's speed)
- run-b-x4 loses completion or more than 2 points of coffee (latency changes outcomes, not just time)
- run-b-x4 is no slower (the decision loop is not on the critical path at all)

**It is supported if:**

- run-b finishes within 10 s of instant, run-b-x4 takes at least 10 s longer, and neither loses completion or coffee

## Why we asked

NEXT_MODEL.md argues speed is not today's constraint (p95 397 ms against a 6 s timeout). That was an argument from one run, not a measurement: nothing has ever varied latency and watched the world.

- docs/NEXT_MODEL.md §3: latency median / p95 289 / 397 ms

## Design

- **Independent variable:** `latency`: Simulated decision latency for a local provider: `instant`, `run-b` (resampled from live run B's recorded latencies) or `run-b-xK` (K times slower).
- **Held fixed:** `provider` = `baseline`
- **Episode:** from the start of After Hours until the midnight transmission is received (at most 900 simulated s)
- **Runs:** 10 per condition; run *i* of every condition uses seed 1 + *i* (paired)

| Condition | Setting | Provider | Runs | Repeats exactly? |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | --: | :-- |
| `instant` Instant (a scripted policy's own speed) | `latency` = `instant` | baseline | 10 / 10 | yes (deterministic) |
| `run-b` Run B's recorded latencies | `latency` = `run-b` | baseline | 10 / 10 | no (seeded or live) |
| `run-b-x4` Four times slower | `latency` = `run-b-x4` | baseline | 10 / 10 | no (seeded or live) |

- **`run-b-x10`**: runs ended by goal ×5, time_budget ×5; diagnosis: stalled in 20, stuck in 5. Example, `run-b-x10-01`: 99.9 s and 19 decisions without task progress (most: drive_to ×12, navigate_to ×3, interact ×2).

## Evidence level

- **Built:** definition `decision-latency` validates (c77186a53c6e).
- **Ran:** 40 runs recorded, 40 with a verified trace.
- **Observed:** the tables below, measured from those traces.
- **Reproduced:** `instant` deterministic: 10 runs, identical measurements yes; `run-b` 10 seeded replicates; `run-b-x4` 10 seeded replicates; `run-b-x10` 10 seeded replicates.
- **Established:** never claimed by this tool. It would need the result to hold on another build, on other days and, for a model, on other model versions.

## Limitations

- The scripted baseline never needs to rethink, so this measures what latency costs a decider that is always right; a model that corrects itself pays latency once per correction.
- Simulated latency is resampled from one live run and scaled; real tail latency may be heavier.
- The scripted baseline is a hand-written policy, not a model: its results describe what the world does to that policy, not what any model would do.
- Metrics are computed in the client from the session's own records; nothing here is server-attested.
